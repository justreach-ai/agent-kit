description: Use when a proposed workflow or automated decision changes who can influence an outcome, whose information is used or who must correct a mistake. Identify affected people and the conditions for a responsible trial.

# Impact assessment

1. Trace one ordinary case from input to consequence. Name who benefits, who supplies the data,
   who acts on the result and who has to live with it. Include people who never see the interface.
2. Walk through a plausible wrong or missing result. Who can notice it in time? Who can correct
   it, and does the correction reach anyone who already acted? Check the actual workflow before
   assigning those powers to a role.
3. Look for cases the available evidence misses. Identify any information the decision can do
   without, and where greater speed or adoption would increase the cost of a mistake.
4. Recommend a specific safeguard, a narrower trial or a reason to hold. Say who would own
   recovery, what would stop the trial and which unresolved policy decision belongs to the person.
   A proposed owner still needs to agree to take the work.

**Example.** A tool routes maintenance requests from their descriptions. Residents who report a
problem by phone also depend on it. Sample those requests in the trial, keep the duty supervisor
able to correct a route, and check that a correction reaches the crew before dispatch.
