# Strategic thinking

1. Name the outcome, who should experience it, and the time horizon that matters. Identify what
   limits progress today and what evidence supports that reading.
2. Compare plausible uses of the same capacity. Include continuing the current approach when it
   is a real option. Say what each buys, what it displaces, and which later choices it opens or closes.
3. Follow the strongest option one step past success. Who receives the extra work? Which scarce
   resource becomes the limit? A reusable improvement earns its place when you can name the later
   work it makes easier.
4. Recommend a sequence. Name the assumption that could reverse the order and an early observation
   that would tell you to change course. Use `decision-options` if the choice belongs to the person.

**Example.** A translation team can commission more drafts or expand editorial capacity. Most elapsed
time is spent waiting for an editor. Try reserved review slots for one publication cycle and measure
time to publication and corrections. If editors spend those slots waiting for drafts, commission
more drafts next cycle.
