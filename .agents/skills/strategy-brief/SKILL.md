# Strategy brief

1. Start with the choice and what the audience should do differently. Identify the practical
   problem they already recognise.
2. Explain the evidence or constraint behind the choice. Use the observation that separates the chosen path from the
   strongest alternative, and show how the proposed action should improve the outcome.
3. Name what the choice costs or defers. Be clear about the weakest assumption and the signal
   that would justify revisiting it.
4. Close with the next action, its owner if agreed, and when the audience can judge the result.
   Where the direction is still open, use `decision-options` to obtain the choice first.

**Example.** “For the next catalogue update, editors will review changes in small batches. Most
corrections last month arrived after the catalogue had gone to print. Earlier review gives authors
time to act, at the cost of a second editorial slot. The publishing lead will compare late
corrections and editorial hours after the next edition.”
